Frequently Asked Questions on Steamboat Island

Can I remove trees along a marine bluff on Steamboat Island?

Properties along Totten Inlet and Eld Inlet fall under the Thurston County Shoreline Master Program (SMP) and Critical Areas Ordinance (Title 24). Tree roots are vital for stabilizing coastal bluffs. Complete removal often requires an arborist hazard assessment or geotechnical review, while hazard pruning, crown reduction, or windowing for views is frequently allowed without triggering permit violations.

Why are Steamboat Island trees vulnerable to windthrow?

The Steamboat Island peninsula is exposed to unrestricted maritime winds across Puget Sound. During winter rainstorms, shallow topsoils above dense glacial till become completely saturated, causing tall Douglas-firs and hemlocks on exposed bluff edges to experience severe wind leverage and root plate uplift.

How are trees safely dismantled on steep shoreline lots?

Crews use high-line rigging systems, friction brakes, and specialized compact machinery to lower heavy wood without letting debris crash down the bluff into marine waters or damaging septic drain fields.
