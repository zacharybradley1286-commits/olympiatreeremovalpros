Controlled Tree Felling & Technical Dismantling in Olympia

From towering coastal Douglas firs with fungal butt rot to wind-compromised Bigleaf maples hanging over residential roofs, precision sectional dismantling allows trees to be safely taken down in tight urban lots across Olympia, Tumwater, and Lacey without damaging homes, septic tanks, or pristine landscaping.

Pacific Northwest soils—particularly the glacial till and saturated soils common throughout Thurston County—create unique structural challenges for mature canopy trees. During heavy South Sound winter rainfall, root systems can lose soil purchase, resulting in dangerous lean and root plate lifting. Independent, fully licensed local arborists utilize high-capacity rigging blocks, friction brakes, and bucket trucks to dismantle hazardous trees piece-by-piece from the canopy down.

Common Tree Hazards in Thurston County

Laminated Root Rot & Fungal Decay

Phellinus weirii (laminated root rot) and Armillaria frequently weaken the root systems of Douglas firs and Western hemlocks across the Olympia area, causing sudden windthrow even during moderate squalls.

Included Bark & Codominant Stems

Mature Bigleaf maples often develop tight V-shaped crotches with included bark. As trunks expand, structural pressure splits codominant stems, creating catastrophic failure risks over driveways and rooflines.

Ground Saturation & Root Plate Lift

Prolonged November–March rainfall saturates topsoil above dense glacial hardpan, loosening root anchors. Noticeable soil mounding or cracked soil at the root flare signals immediate failure risk.

Olympia Municipal Tree Regulations & Permit Guidance

Before cutting or removing trees on your residential property, it is critical to confirm local municipal tree codes. Tree protection ordinances vary across jurisdictions in Thurston County:

Jurisdiction Exemption Provision Permit Trigger Criteria Applicable Code
City of Olympia Single-family parcels under 2 acres maintaining minimum tree density. Trees >24" DBH (landmark specimens) or critical area/wetland buffers. OMC Ch. 16.60 & 16.56
City of Tumwater Up to 6 trees per parcel per 3 consecutive calendar years. Greenbelts, historic zones, wetland/stream buffers, or commercial sites. TMC Ch. 16.08
City of Lacey Up to 3 trees per 60 consecutive months on single-family lots. More than 3 trees, landmark trees, or designated open space areas. LMC Ch. 14.32
Thurston County Standard rural residential maintenance outside protected buffers. Critical Areas Ordinance (streams, shorelines, bluffs, oak woodlands). Title 24 (CAO)

Always verify current parcel zoning and code requirements with your local jurisdiction prior to initiating felling operations. Independent tree specialists can assist in assessing permit thresholds and preparing hazard documentation.

The 4-Step Precision Tree Removal Process

1. Site & Canopy Hazard Evaluation

A certified specialist evaluates trunk sound, decay pockets, power line clearances, drop zones, and target zones to determine the safest felling or dismantling method.

2. Sectional Canopy Dismantling

Climbers ascend the tree to limb branches from bottom to top, lowering limbs under tension before sectioning the main trunk in manageable firewood or mill lengths.

3. Debris Chipping & Site Cleanup

Brush is processed through commercial high-capacity wood chippers. Firewood rounds can be stacked on-site or hauled away, leaving the grounds raked and clean.

4. Optional Root Flare & Stump Grinding

Tracked hydraulic stump grinders grind the remaining stump and surface roots 6 to 12 inches below grade, preparing the footprint for replanting or lawn restoration.
