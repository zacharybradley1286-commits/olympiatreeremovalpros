Frequently Asked Questions in West Olympia

Why does West Olympia cost more than a flat lot?

Access. On a ravine lot off Cooper Point there is often nowhere to park a chipper near the tree, so brush gets carried out by hand and logs get bucked small enough to move. That is labor, and it is the single biggest line item on a steep-slope removal.

Will removing trees destabilize my slope?

It can. Root systems hold soil on a grade, and clearing a slope-edge stand is how properties start losing ground. Olympia regulates work in geologically hazardous areas for exactly this reason. On slope lots the right plan is often selective removal of the genuinely hazardous trees plus crown reduction on the rest — not a clear cut.

What about trees on the Budd Inlet bluffs?

Bluff-edge trees are exposed to wind with no shelter, and they sit on soils that undercut over time. They fail more often than inland trees of the same size and species. If you have a large conifer within a tree-length of the house on a bluff lot, it is worth an assessment before winter rather than after a storm.
